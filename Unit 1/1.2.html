Analysis & Critical Evaluation by Sebastien

When attempting to make healthy choices, several large barriers can hinder healthy and effective decision making. First, a lack of sufficient information can leave oneself feeling stuck, as the text says that without enough data, "it can feel like you are making a decision without any basis" (page 3). However, when having too much conflicting information, it can overwhelm oneself, resulting in "having so much conflicting information that it is impossible to see 'the wood for the trees'" (page 4). In addition to this, emotional attachments are a big challenge because "people are often very attached to the status quo" (page 4). Biology also works against us since "evolution has led us to naturally make short-term decisions a priority," causing people to heavily focus on immediate comfort instead of long-term well-being (page 9). Since instant gratification feels far more appealing than waiting for results, using and applying a structured decision-making process is required when overcoming such obstacles.

Personal Case Analysis

A friend of mine had demonstrated how important it is to use a structured decision-making process when he decided to change his physical health. After realizing he was overweight, he chose to stop making impulsive choices and fully committed to cutting the junk food and going to the gym. His decision was mostly individual, as he decided to hold himself accountable instead of relying on other people to make the choices for him. Even though it must have been very difficult to break his old habits, he had recognized that "solutions with immediate strong benefits strike us as much more attractive than solutions with less immediate results," so he pushed past the desire for short-term comfort (page 9). He was able to create healthy patterns so he could do it without even thinking, allowing for him to achieve his goal of becoming more fit and losing weight.

Alternative Decisions

There were a couple of alternative decisions he could have made instead. One alternative decision would have been trying a fast crash diet to lose weight extremely quickly. But that probably would not have lasted long-term because it would not have made a real routine, making it far easier to return to old habits.

Another alternative choice would have been making it a collaborative decision rather than individual by getting a group of friends to join a gym class together. While that might give extra motivation, relying on other people's schedules can make it pretty easy to drop out if everyone else stops going due to schedules not lining up, which is why making it an individual decision worked best for him.

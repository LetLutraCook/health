Analysis: Two Influences with Negative Health Impacts & Preventive Actions

1. Technology & Peer Pressure (Excessive Screen Time)

Negative Impact: A clear negative influence recently experienced involved excessive screen time driven by teammates pressuring me to stay logged on and keep playing video games late into the evening (pages 3, 8, iBook part one). This digital peer pressure led to late nights, reduced physical recovery, and sleep deprivation, which negatively affected daytime energy and concentration (page 3, iBook part two).

Preventive Behaviors: To prevent this negative impact in the future, I could have established firm personal boundaries by setting a strict cutoff time for gaming, communicating my schedule to my teammates ahead of time, and prioritizing sleep hygiene over digital social demands (pages 4–5, 10–11, iBook part one).

2. Dietary Habits & Peer Behavioral Influences

Negative Impact: Peer dynamics can also encourage extreme or unhealthy eating habits during social gatherings when group members push playful overindulgence (pages 4–5, iBook part one).

Preventive Behaviors: Recognizing these behavioral cues and relying on personal health values helps individuals intervene and make health-conscious choices rather than following unhealthy group momentum (pages 10–11, iBook part one).

Synthesis of Unit Health Standards

Family, Culture, Peers & Environmental Factors

Families and peer groups lay the foundational framework for health through daily habits, social expectations, and behavioral modeling (pages 2–3, iBook part one). Managing digital and social environments ensures that these influences remain positive (pages 6–7, iBook part one).

Norms, Values, and Behavioral Impact

Core values serve as an internal compass that guides health choices against peer pressure, while social norms dictate perceived expectations (pages 9–11, iBook part one). Correcting misperceived norms allows individuals to resist unhealthy behaviors and align with health-enhancing lifestyles (pages 4, 10, iBook part one).

Influencing Others & Adapted Communication

Supporting others involves active intervention, open dialogue, and encouraging moderation in daily routines and nutrition (pages 2–3, 8, iBook part two). Adapting health messages ensures effective communication across different social scenarios (page 4, 8, iBook part two).

Consequences of Harassment, Intimidation & Bullying (ORC 3313.60)

Harassment and intimidation cause severe emotional and psychological trauma that outlasts physical harm (pages 6–7, iBook part two). Preventing these negative consequences requires assertive communication and involving support networks (page 7, iBook part two).

Conclusion & Action Plan

By evaluating screen time limits, managing peer pressures, and actively supporting friends toward moderation, individuals can make conscious choices that promote personal, family, and community well-being (pages 10–12, iBook part one; pages 2–4, iBook part two). Active advocacy and self-awareness ensure long-term health resilience and positive growth.

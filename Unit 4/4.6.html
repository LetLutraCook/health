Analysis & Critical Evaluation by Sebastien

Harassment, intimidation, and bullying carry severe consequences that deeply impact young people during their development (page 6, iBook part two). A bully is defined as someone who is overbearing or cruel with the specific aim of causing embarrassment and humiliation (page 6, iBook part two). Harassment involves systematically bothering someone to the point where the environment becomes dangerous and is aimed at intimidation and domination (page 6, iBook part two). Importantly, bullying is not restricted to physical harm; non-physical forms such as emotional, verbal, or electronic bullying can be just as traumatizing, with psychological effects that linger long after physical signs disappear (pages 6–7, iBook part two).

Consequences of Harassment, Intimidation, and Bullying

The consequences of these actions manifest as persistent fear, anxiety, and severe emotional distress for the victim. Because bullies intentionally target individuals who appear vulnerable, dealing with harassment requires proactive defense techniques, such as exhibiting good posture, maintaining eye contact, staying in public areas with witnesses, keeping a strong support system of friends, and immediately notifying authority figures (page 7, iBook part two).

Personal Situation Analysis: Addressing Harassment and Bullying

A situation where bullying or intimidation was witnessed occurred in a school hallway when a peer was subjected to repeated verbal taunts and exclusion by a dominant group.

What was the outcome? The outcome was that the targeted student became isolated and withdrawn, experiencing high stress and discomfort in school common areas before intervention took place (pages 6–7, iBook part two).

Could it have been handled differently? Yes, this situation could have been handled more effectively by actively stepping in with friends to provide a support system, refusing to allow the behavior to go unnoticed, and notifying an authority figure right away to ensure the safety and well-being of the student (page 7, iBook part two).

Analysis & Critical Evaluation by Sebastien

Schools, media, and technology exert a powerful influence on adolescent development, personal health practices, and behavior (pages 6–8, iBook part one). As the primary institutional context for young people, schools affect risk behaviors through structural characteristics, classroom microcontexts, and peer networks that shape student connectedness and safety (pages 6–7, iBook part one). Furthermore, media exposure plays a major role; theories such as social learning theory suggest that when individuals see behaviors portrayed positively, they tend to imitate them, while cultivation theory indicates that media perceptions can supersede real-world norms (page 8, iBook part one). In addition, excessive screen time and technology usage promote sedentary lifestyles and increase obesity risks (page 3, iBook part two).

Evaluating School, Media, and Technology on Health Practices

Each of these environmental factors interacts to shape health behaviors. Schools implement strategies ranging from security and counseling to climate improvement programs to influence social and behavioral outcomes (page 7, iBook part one). Meanwhile, media sources and fast-evolving technology continually impact teenage nutrition, body image, and daily physical activity levels (pages 8–9, iBook part one; page 3, iBook part two).

Personal Situation Analysis: Technology Impact on Health

A clear situation where technology affected personal health practices involved managing daily screen time and its tendency to encroach upon physical activity and rest. Spending extended hours on computers and mobile devices created a more sedentary routine, which initially threatened to reduce overall daily movement and interfere with sleep hygiene.

Was the effect positive or negative? This was primarily a negative effect when left unmonitored, as excessive screen time promotes physical inactivity and increases health risks (page 3, iBook part two).

Explain: Recognizing this risk prompted a deliberate adjustment to unplug regularly, limit recreational screen use, and reintroduce structured physical exercise and outdoor activity, successfully restoring a healthy balance (pages 2–3, iBook part two).

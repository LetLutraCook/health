Personal values and perceived societal norms exert a powerful influence over individual health practices, choices, and daily behaviors (pages 9–10, iBook part one). In personal terms, "values" represent the fundamental principles, moral standards, and core beliefs that an individual holds dear, which guide their decisions on what is right and beneficial. On the other hand, "norms" refer to the accepted standards, behaviors, and social expectations within a peer group or broader community (pages 9–10, iBook part one). Perceptions of these norms heavily shape actions; for instance, media sources and societal messages can make risky behaviors appear more prevalent and attractive than they actually are (pages 9–10, iBook part one). Furthermore, as adolescents grow into their teen years, an increased awareness of societal laws and community expectations helps them think more critically about the consequences of illegal or risky activities (pages 10–11, iBook part one).

Analyzing Norms and Personal Values on Health

When individuals misperceive peer norms—such as assuming that risky behaviors are the majority standard—they are more likely to adopt those same unhealthy habits (page 4, iBook part one). Conversely, utilizing accurate societal norms and grounding decisions in positive personal values enables adolescents to resist negative peer pressure, align with legal standards, and foster self-compassion, which research shows leads to greater resiliency and success (pages 10–12, iBook part one).

Personal Situation Analysis: Formulating a Health-Enhancing Message on Vaping

A clear personal situation where accurate norms were utilized to formulate a health-enhancing message involved addressing the widespread trend of youth vaping. Observing that many peers perceived vaping as an inevitable or universal habit driven by peer pressure and media exposure, it became clear that the perceived social norm was heavily distorted.

By looking at actual health statistics and data showing that the majority of teens actually choose not to vape, an accurate peer norm was established (page 10, iBook part one). Grounding the response in personal values of health and self-respect, a health-enhancing message was formulated and shared within the peer group. Highlighting true statistics rather than exaggerated social pressures helped shift the conversation, reinforcing positive choices and proving that choosing health is both normal and empowering.

Personal Wellness & Behavioral Improvement Presentation by Sebastien

Taking individual responsibility for personal health involves making conscious daily choices, avoiding major health risks, and maintaining balanced physical habits. While family genetics play a role in health conditions, individual behaviors are often just as important for overall wellness (page 3). By identifying areas for personal growth and implementing target behavioral changes, we can protect our body systems, avoid risks like drug addiction, and improve our long-term health (page 2, 7).

5 Personal Health Behaviors for Improvement

1. Increasing Daily Physical Activity (Standard 7.12.1 & 7.12.2)

Concept & Importance: Lack of physical activity is a key risk factor for major health issues like heart disease and diabetes (page 2). Adding small daily movements burns energy and reduces chronic disease risks (page 4).

Personal Example: An example of this that I have experienced was when I felt like I was sitting down for too many hours straight working on computer projects. I realized I was barely getting any movement during long days at my desk. To fix this behavior, I plan to schedule two 15-minute walking breaks during my study sessions and take the stairs whenever possible (page 4).

2. Analyzing Nutrition Labels & Cutting High-Fat Foods (Standard H.1.13)

Concept & Importance: Reading nutrition labels allows consumers to make informed choices, checking calories, sodium, and saturated fats to avoid excess weight and high blood pressure (page 3, 10).

Personal Example: An example of this that I have experienced was when I grabbed quick processed snacks during busy afternoons without ever looking at the serving sizes or saturated fat contents. To improve, I will actively analyze food labels before buying snacks, making sure to replace high-fat options with fruits and vegetables to get higher nutritional quality per calorie (page 3, 10).

3. Improving Daily Hydration Habits (Standard 7.12.2)

Concept & Importance: Proper hydration is vital for body system functions. Swapping out sugary beverages for water or non-fat milk reduces empty calories and prevents metabolic strain (page 4).

Personal Example: An example of this that I have experienced was relying heavily on high-caffeine and sweetened energy drinks to get through long afternoons, which left me feeling dehydrated and crash-prone. My goal is to substitute water or unsweetened drinks for sugary beverages throughout the day to keep my body properly hydrated (page 4).

4. Avoiding Risky Substances & Protecting Body Systems (Standard H.1.3 & 7.12.3)

Concept & Importance: Substance use causes immediate and long-term damage to major body systems. Tobacco elevates heart rate and damages lungs (page 6), while alcohol acts as a depressant that impairs coordination and judgment (page 5). Addiction is a chronic brain disease that alters brain circuits controlling self-control and decision-making (page 7).

Personal Example: An example of this that I have experienced was being in social environments where vaping or alcohol were present and feeling subtle social pressure. To reduce health risks to myself and others, I choose to refrain completely from tobacco, alcohol, and drug misuse, keeping my body systems healthy and avoiding the risk of addiction (page 3, 7).

5. Evaluating Dietary Supplements Safely (Standard H.1.11)

Concept & Importance: Dietary supplements cannot replace a healthy diet and can pose risks like organ toxicity or negative interactions with medications (page 11, 12).

Personal Example: An example of this that I have experienced was considering workout supplements and protein powders advertised online without checking their safety or ingredients. My actionable goal is to consult a healthcare provider before starting any new supplement and to rely on nutrient-rich whole foods first (page 11, 12).

Understanding Addiction & Treatment Options (ORC 3313.60)

Addiction to alcohol, tobacco, opiates, and other drugs is a treatable disease that alters brain functions related to memory, reward, and behavior control (page 7). Because addiction changes how nerve cells process information, stopping use requires long-term care rather than short-term willpower (page 7).

Key Treatment Options:

  • Medications: Used during detoxification to reduce withdrawal symptoms and reestablish normal brain function to prevent cravings and relapse (page 8).
  • Behavioral Treatments: Outpatient individual or group counseling helps patients modify attitudes, develop coping skills, and stay drug-free (page 8).
  • Residential Programs: Therapeutic communities provide long-term structured environments (6 to 12 months) for severe cases (page 9).
  • Community Resources: Programs like Mental Health Addiction Services of Ohio and Start Talking! offer accessible support networks (page 9).

Cultural Cuisines & Environmental Factors (H.6.3 & H.6.7)

Nutritional values and food ingredients vary across ethnic and cultural cuisines, providing diverse sources of essential vitamins and minerals (page 12). However, environmental issues significantly impact the food supply and quality:

  • Industrial Monoculture & Soil Depletion: Heavy reliance on growing single crops like corn and soy depletes soil nutrients and increases pesticide usage, polluting water systems (page 12).
  • Nutritional Decline in Produce: Transporting processed foods long distances and soil degradation have caused vitamins and minerals in fruits and vegetables (such as iron, vitamin C, and calcium) to drop significantly over recent decades (page 13).
  • Body Detoxification: Environmental toxins accumulate in the food chain, making our body's internal detoxification systems (liver, kidneys, intestines) critical for processing chemical exposures (page 13).

Conclusion & Summary

By taking individual responsibility for daily choices—such as reading nutrition labels, increasing physical activity, staying hydrated, avoiding drugs and tobacco, and understanding environmental impacts on food—we can build sustainable habits that prevent disease and enhance overall life quality (page 3, 4, 7). Focusing on specific, realistic behavior changes ensures long-term success and health resilience (page 4).
